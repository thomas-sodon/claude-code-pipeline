# Spec: ask the person, never choose for them

Work tree: `/home/user/ccp-work` (a git worktree of the pipeline; edit ONLY there). Never edit `/home/user/claude-code-pipeline`
(reviewers are reading it), the idea-skill and product checkouts attached read-only as evidence.
Never run git commands that change state (no add, commit, branch, checkout, stash, push): the orchestrator commits.

Why: a real product run, in a private repository, showed the pipeline choosing for the person. Its Intake asked 1 question and took 9
assumptions (licence variant, scope, name check, region, thresholds, accounts); the generated brief's price, licence and
platforms went into the docs as "(brief)" facts nobody confirmed; two choices the person had made binding were overturned
because "the source wins"; ten open decisions were parked in the Bootstrap gate; the authors invented provenance marks in
D-record headings; the person's decisions lived in a local-only file rewritten by hand in every container; and questions
arrived one per workflow pass. The owner's rule: "The person resolves every contradiction, assumption and decision."

House style: plain declarative sentences, "the person" (never "the user" for the product owner), no em-dash flourishes beyond
what the file already uses, commands keep their long one-paragraph-per-rule shape. Nothing under `.claude/` may contain the
strings `maestro`, `/home/` or `/opt/` (CI greps for them). Keep every existing behaviour not named here.

## 1. Vocabulary (use these exact terms)

- **blocking question**: one only the person can answer: what they intend, a requirement or constraint the source leaves
  out or states two incompatible ways, a trade-off that is theirs to make, a conflict between what the person said (the
  framing, an answer, a decision a doc records as theirs) and the source or the author's research, or a blocker whose way
  forward is their call; and whose plausible answers lead to a different design, promise, cost or legal exposure.
- **confirmation**: a question about a *person-owned choice* that already has a value nobody confirmed with the person:
  the source's value when the source was drafted for the person (a `new-software-idea` brief is: its header has
  `**Verdict:**` and `**Assumed builder:**` fields, and that skill never interviews the person), an item the framing or a
  panel synthesis lists as open, recommended or not yet decided, or the default the author would otherwise take. It is asked
  in the same batch as the blocking questions, with that value as the recommended option, and marked as a confirmation
  (workflow schema: `confirm: true`).
- **person-owned choices** (canonical list, same order everywhere): who the product is for and what its first release leaves
  out; platforms and distribution, hosted or self-run; price, plans, the revenue model, and anything that costs money or
  opens an account in the person's name (a host, a paid API, a billing provider, a store); the licence and its variant, and
  the legal and compliance posture; what personal data is collected, how long it is kept and who receives it; names (product,
  package, domain, organization or bundle id); the repository's owner and visibility; stop and go criteria, thresholds and
  dates; and a stack, provider or environment the person must run or pay for themselves.
- **yours (the author's), never asked**: a choice inside a component that an invariant, a recorded decision or an official
  doc settles (a library, a layout, an internal name, a test tool, a version), and anything the tree, `CLAUDE.md`, the docs
  or desk research can settle.
- **assumption**: every other choice the inputs did not settle. It is never silent: it is tagged in the doc (section 2) and
  listed in the report or result.

Rules that go with them (state each where the file's stage can meet it):

- R1 **Ask in one batch, early.** Every blocking question and confirmation in one pass, before writing.
- R2 **The person outranks the source.** What the person said is binding. Where the source, the tree or research contradicts
  it, pick no side: it is a blocking question naming both. (Old wording "binding unless a source fact contradicts it" and
  "the source wins" goes; the source still wins over a panel synthesis the person has not confirmed.)
- R3 **A choice the person can make now is a question**: never a gate, never a D-record whose Choice awaits confirmation,
  never something left for acceptance. A `decision` gate holds only what waits on evidence the plan produces, on finished
  work or on an outside party; a `research` gate only what needs data, an account, a paid search or a legal opinion (a store,
  registry or register with a public search is desk research, recorded with its result and date).
- R4 **Never change what the person settled.** An answer, the framing, or a D-record tagged `person` is changed only by a
  newer answer from the person; a reason to change it is a blocking question. Reviewers report such a change as a `bug`
  whose fix is the question to ask, with options.
- R5 **Name things by title.** A question, its options and the recommendation name milestones, steps and decisions by
  title; a number alone is never enough (numbers move between revisions).
- R6 **Proposed values.** A value the author proposes (a threshold, a retention period, a price) is labelled `(proposed)` and
  says where the person fixes it: a confirmation now when it is a promise they own and can answer from what they know, else
  the gate whose Acceptance records it.

## 2. D-record provenance tag

Every D-record heading in an architecture or proposal ends with one closed tag:

    ### D3: Data store (assumed)
    ### D7: Free-check input (person, 2026-11-05)
    ### D12: Auth (person, 2026-11-12, architecture.q2)
    ### D1: Language and framework (source)

- `(person, <YYYY-MM-DD>[, <ref>])`: the person chose it; the date they said it; `<ref>` is where: an answer id
  (`intake.q2`, `intake.d3`), `framing`, a gate (`roadmap.md#4`), or `edit`. Binding (R4).
- `(source)`: the source states it AND either the source is the person's own writing or the choice is not person-owned.
  A person-owned choice taken from a source drafted for the person is never `(source)`: it is a confirmation, and becomes
  `(person, ...)` once confirmed.
- `(assumed)`: the author chose. For a person-owned choice this is allowed only when the run proceeds on assumptions (the
  invoking prompt says so; workflows: `on_question: 'assume'`); otherwise the choice is asked first.

`(proposed)` stays the mark for a value (R6). Authors write no other provenance mark.

Lint (`.claude/scripts/proposal-lint.py`), architecture and proposal docs, headings `### D<N>: ...` under `## Decisions`:
- warning `decision_untagged`: ONE warning per doc, at the first untagged record, listing the untagged ids
  (older docs keep passing: warnings never fail the lint).
- warning `bad_decision_tag`: per record whose trailing parenthetical starts with `person`, `source` or `assumed` but is not
  one of the closed forms (for example `(person)` with no date, `(person's decision, 2026-11-05)`).
- `--json` gains `decisions: [{id, n, title, line, by, date, ref}]` (`by`: person | source | assumed | '' when untagged).
- new read-only mode `--decisions` (JSON, mutually exclusive with the other modes): `{doc, kind, decisions: [...],
  unconfirmed: [the records whose by is assumed or ''], proposed: [{line, text}]}` where `proposed` lists every non-fenced
  line containing `(proposed`. Works on any kind (a roadmap has no decisions; its proposed lines are listed). Exit 0 unless
  the doc cannot be read (2).

## 3. State-branch files (new)

| Path | Written by | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `design/answers.json` | `brief-to-proposals` (save-answers stage) | every answer the run was given: `{ source, answers: [{ id, question, answer, date }] }` |
| `context/builder-context.md` | `/start-product`, `/handoff` | the builder framing, ONLY when the person consented to store it there |

The builder context holds who builds and the limits (time, money, skills, target) only. A decision the person makes is
never kept there: it travels as an answer (`intake.d<N>` when no workflow question asked it) and lands in the doc as a
`(person, ...)` tag.
